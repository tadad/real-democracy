Jay Red Hawk (whatever his real name may be) rides truer than any tribal bureaucrat.
You see him on horseback at dusk: feathered, bare-chested, bow in hand.
He looks like the Red Man, moves like the Red Man, speaks in the cadence of ancient memory.

Jay Red Hawk may not be Lakota by blood.
He’s not reenacting a stale tribal dance, or a government-sanctioned cultural performance: he is living as art.
That he is “pretending” makes it better: it means anyone can do it.
Every American can look to the Red Man, not as a symbol of trauma, but as a spirit to embody.
When the Great Plains become the American Serengeti again, every American must wear the feathers, ride the horse, and take up the bow.

Jay Red Hawk is not a Lakota, he is a Dream, and in this tired empire, dreamers are more valuable than descendants.
