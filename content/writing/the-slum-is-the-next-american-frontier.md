The reaction against NIMBY's, sluggish bureaucracy, high rents, government waste, and the overall decline in quality of life in North America has led to a reaction:  people calling for the creation of new cities, societies, and countries.
Seasteading tried to open the oceans by making "Seapods".
Liberland tried to take over some "unclaimed" land in the Balkans.
Praxis raised $500M to build a new city, though we are still waiting for them to lay the first brick.
[California Forever](https://californiaforever.com/) is trying to relocate people from SF to a new nearby settlement in Solano County.

The rub is that moving everyone somewhere new is a lot harder than scaling an existing settlement.
The question is: how can we scale our existing cities without having to go through NIMBY boomer town hall disagreements and slogging through local city politics?
The solution is to just start building things.
NIMBYism is a first world problem, and the solution is to become third world.

## The Minimum Viable City

As De Gaulle once put it: "Brazil is the country of the future and always will be."
Rio went through a "housing crisis" over 50 years ago, and instead of waiting for more high rises to be built, they erected new homes on the outskirts of the city where no one was looking - usually in flood zones, hills, etc. - any place where the more legit real estate developers and land owners didn't care about.
Eventually more and more people moved there and now they are legitimate neighborhoods with tens of thousansd of people.
Last November I saw a 9 (nine) story building in Rochina made of nothing but bricks and cement.
I also saw the ruins of a house that collapsed.
A few people died - that's life.
If someone needs a new place to rent, they just can build a new floor, no regulatory approval required.
I can't think of anything more YIMBY than that.

## Let's Build a Favela in San Francisco
You can just do things.
You can just build a house.
People are already living in tents and cardboard boxes in American cities: building permanent homes for these people is an improvement in the standard of living for everyone.
The homeless get some permanent housing, and the rest of SF doesn't have to step over needles and feces on their way to work.

Cheap rent has it's own second order positives: suddenly artists, aspiring entrepreneurs, and young people can afford to leave the burbs for a dense urban area.
I know a guy that lived in a tent in Marin to pursue his dream of becoming a founder.
Imagine how much better it would've been if he didn't have to cross the Golden Gate Bridge just to meet more tech bros.

In a few generations the SF favela could become gentrified and look like the Amalfi coast.
These days, the most expensive favelas in Rio are basically tourist destinations with hotels, restaurants, and bars.
It actually doesn't take that much time and effort to improve these places once they're established.
Singapore was once just bunch of opium dens on a rock.

The arrival of the slum and the latinification of America is upon us.

The only way forward is the favela.
