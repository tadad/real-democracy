I think it's best to understand Nietzsche's _Ubermensch_ through vector spaces.
A vector is an object with both magnitude and direction, represented as an ordered list of numbers.
A vector space is a mathematical structure made up of vectors that can be added together and multiplied by scalars (numbers), and the results always remain within the space.
The space has a certain dimension, 2d, 3d, 4d, ... Nd.
The dimensionality of the space is defined by the "basis," for a 3d space: [1,0.0] [0,1,0] and [0,0,1].
When a linear transformation is applied to a vector space (like rotating, scaling, or shearing), eigenvectors are the special vectors that don’t change direction under that transformation.
